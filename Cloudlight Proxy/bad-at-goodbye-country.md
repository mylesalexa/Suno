## Researching the trend
- **The chart right now:** country is having a rare moment — multiple country songs sitting inside the
  overall Hot 100's top five at once, something that's essentially never happened before. The songs
  driving it are overwhelmingly **male/female duets** built around messy, honest, still-in-it relationship
  dynamics — not clean breakup songs or clean love songs, but the complicated middle: two people who can't
  quite stay together and can't quite stay apart.
- **The production trend:** a genuine traditionalist-revival is happening alongside it — fiddle, pedal
  steel, raw unpolished vocals, red-dirt and honky-tonk instincts are back in favor after years of pure
  pop-country gloss. But the songs that are actually charting aren't purist throwbacks — they quietly layer
  a modern atmospheric synth pad or subtle electronic texture underneath the traditional instrumentation,
  so it reads as classic country on top and radio-modern underneath.
- **The lyrical trend:** authentic storytelling over polish. Resilience, identity, and love in its
  complicated real forms — not the tidy version.
- **The translation for this song:** a male/female duet about two people who keep breaking up and keep
  coming back — the exact emotional lane the chart is rewarding right now — built in that
  traditionalist-plus-atmospheric production blend, with a hook simple enough to become the title.

---

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** two people who are genuinely good together and genuinely terrible at ending it — every time
one of them tries to leave, the other one calls, and they're right back where they started. Not a
reconciliation song and not a breakup song — the whole point is that it never actually resolves, because
that's the joke and the truth of it. **The hook:** "We're so good together, so bad at goodbye" — simple,
singable, and it does double duty as both the chorus and the whole premise of the song. **Why it fits the
trend instead of chasing it generically:** it's built as a true duet with alternating verses (his side,
then her side, same story from both angles) rather than one lead voice with backup harmony — that's the
specific structural thing separating the songs currently topping the chart from a normal solo ballad.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** traditionalist-revival modern country duet — fiddle and pedal steel up front, acoustic guitar,
a subtle atmospheric synth pad underneath for modern radio texture, raw and honest (not over-polished)
vocals. Mid-tempo, built for a chorus that lands emotionally, not a dance floor.

- **Tempo:** ~**92 BPM** (mid-tempo, unhurried, built for a big singable chorus).
- **Key:** **A major** (warm, not overly bright — sits well under both a male and female vocal range).
- **Vocal:** true **male/female duet** — alternating verses (his side, her side), full harmony on the
  chorus and bridge. Raw, honest, unpolished delivery. Clean.

## ⭐ Best Suno prompt
```
Traditionalist modern country duet, 92 BPM, key of A, fiddle, pedal steel, acoustic guitar, atmospheric synth pad, raw male and female leads, tight harmony chorus, radio-ready production
```
**Tip:** the whole trend this song is chasing lives in two tags working together — `fiddle, pedal steel`
for the traditionalist revival and `atmospheric synth pad` for the modern radio layer underneath it. Don't
drop either one: too much fiddle with no pad reads as a straight throwback, and too much pad with no fiddle
loses the authenticity the chart is rewarding right now. Make sure Suno renders the `[Verse 1 — Him]` /
`[Verse 2 — Her]` tags as genuinely distinct voices, and keep the `Him:` / `Her:` / `Both:` bridge tags
intact so it plays as a real back-and-forth, not one voice reading both parts.

## Variations
- **Bigger emotional swing:** tag the final chorus `(key change up a half step, bigger production)` for an
  even bigger radio moment.
- **Stripped alternate:** drop the synth pad entirely and pull tempo to ~78 BPM for an acoustic, front-porch
  version of the same song.
- **Title/hook swaps:** "So Bad at Goodbye," "Porch Light's Still On," "We Just Say Goodnight," "Back for
  More."
