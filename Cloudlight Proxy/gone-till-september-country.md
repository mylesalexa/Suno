# Gone Till September

*One-line concept: the exact euphoric second you clock out for the summer — phone in the glovebox, badge
on the dash, "out of office" for good — and the whole world turns to lake water and backroads.*

**Strongest angle:** the **clock-out** is the hook. Most summer songs start *at* the lake; this one starts
at the *time clock* and detonates the instant freedom hits. The chant "Gone till September" works as a
mic-drop on the boss, a caption, and a crowd shout — it's a calendar everyone already feels in their bones.
Male lead, windows-down, fist-out-the-window joy from the first downbeat.

---

## Why it's distinct from the catalog
Not a lake-list (Lakeside), not a fling (Tan Lines), not a town/road song (Every Town's Got One, Dirt Road
Disco). This is the **freedom-from-the-grind** moment itself — punch out → throw the phone → gone. The
emotional engine is *release*, not scenery. The lake is just where the release is pointed.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** big, optimistic modern country summer anthem — windows-down, tailgate, festival-sized. Acoustic
+ bright electric, fiddle stabs, banjo roll, stomp-clap groove, gang vocals. Verses conversational and
grinning; chorus explodes; post-chorus is a pure wordless crowd chant.

- **Tempo:** ~**126 BPM** — driving, joyful, fist-pump backbeat.
- **Key:** **E major** (bright), lift to **F#** for the final chorus.
- **Vocal:** **male lead** — easygoing twang in the verses, wide-open and shouting-grinning on the hook.
  Stacked gang vocals + claps on "gone till September" and the "hey-oh" chant. Clean, no autotune.
  *(Female-lead swap noted at the bottom.)*
