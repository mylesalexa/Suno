# Hurry Hard (Bonspiel Night) *(modern country-pop dance — curling bonspiel party anthem)*

*The bonspiel-weekend sibling to "Ten Bucks In (Going Social)" — same rented-hall, small-town-party DNA,
turned loose on curling club culture instead of a wedding fundraiser. A whole Saturday of draws and ends
out on the ice, then the clubhouse bar takes over and the whole rink parties in their curling socks. "Hurry
hard" — the skip's own call to sweep — doubles as the party's entire battle cry. Cranked up, energetic,
not a novelty song. Clean. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Brief:** a companion party anthem to "Ten Bucks In," same energy, cranked up — this time about a
> curling bonspiel. Deep, real curling vocabulary (hack, hog line, house, button, eight-ender) doing the
> same job the legion-hall specifics did for the social song.

---

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** a bonspiel — a curling tournament weekend — where the actual curling (draws, ends, sweeping,
a last-rock steal) is only half the story; the real legend gets made after, when the whole club piles into
the clubhouse bar still in their curling socks and turns the trophy presentation into an all-night party.
**Why it's a fresh angle, not a repeat of "Ten Bucks In":** curling has its own dense, specific, almost
comically Canadian vocabulary — hack, hog line, house, button, rings, the skip calling shots, an eight-ender
— which does exactly what Texas-Mickey/toonie/meat-draw did for the social song: makes it feel like a real
place, not a generic party. And **"hurry hard"** — literally what a skip screams at the sweepers — is a
ready-made chant hook nobody's used yet.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** modern country-pop dance — four-on-the-floor, gritty electric guitar riff, banjo plucks over a
club beat, slap bass, stomp-clap groove, punchy live drums with an 808 layer, confident male lead, tight
harmony stacks, huge gang-vocal chorus, festival country swagger, polished radio mix. Energetic, not
novelty — cranked up past its sibling.

- **Tempo:** ~**130 BPM** (four-on-the-floor, pushed a notch harder than "Ten Bucks In").
- **Vocal:** confident **male lead** — tight harmony stacks, massive gang-vocal chorus.
