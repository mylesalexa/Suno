# Hurry Hard (Bonspiel Night) *(modern country-pop dance — curling bonspiel party anthem)*

*The bonspiel-weekend sibling to "Ten Bucks In (Going Social)" — same small-town-party DNA, but rebuilt with
its own shape after the first draft leaned too hard on the social song's exact chorus scaffold and
breakdown device. This version uses curling's actual **call-and-response** (a skip really does shout single
curling calls that the team answers) as the chorus architecture, and swaps the countdown-then-drop
breakdown for a hushed "last rock" moment that resolves into a crowd roar instead. Same family, different
shape. Clean. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Brief:** a companion party anthem to "Ten Bucks In," same energy — but the first draft copied that
> song's chorus pattern (`X — verb / X — verb ... Everybody, everybody — verb / We're goin' X tonight`) and
> its exact "count-in, beat slams back in, exclaim!" breakdown almost move for move. Revised so it sounds
> like the same family without being a reskin.

---

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** a bonspiel — a curling tournament weekend — where the actual curling (draws, ends, sweeping,
a last-rock steal) is only half the story; the real legend gets made after, when the whole club piles into
the clubhouse bar still in their curling socks and turns the trophy presentation into an all-night party.
**Why it's a fresh angle, not a repeat of "Ten Bucks In":** curling has its own dense, specific, almost
comically Canadian vocabulary — hack, hog line, house, button, rings, the skip calling shots, an eight-ender
— which does exactly what Texas-Mickey/toonie/meat-draw did for the social song: makes it feel like a real
place, not a generic party. **What actually makes the structure different this time:** a curling skip
genuinely shouts single-word calls ("Sweep!", "Clean!", "Line!") that the team answers with action — so the
chorus is built as real **call-and-response** instead of a repeated chant line, and the big tension moment
is a hushed, count-free "watch the rock glide" hold that resolves into a roar, not a spoken countdown into
a beat drop.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** modern country-pop dance — four-on-the-floor, gritty electric guitar riff, banjo plucks over a
club beat, a **broom-sweep percussion texture** (the actual "swish" of a curling broom used as a rhythmic
element — this song's own sonic fingerprint), confident male lead in **call-and-response** with a gang-vocal
crowd, festival country swagger, polished radio mix. Energetic, not novelty.

- **Tempo:** ~**130 BPM** (four-on-the-floor, same crank as before).
- **Vocal:** confident **male lead / skip voice** calling single curling terms, answered by a
  **gang-vocal crowd** — true call-and-response, not a single chanted line.
