# Lakeside

*A feel-good country summer anthem — male lead, built to shout along to with the windows down. All the
lake classics: kids catching turtles, boating and tubing, cannonballs off the rock, ice cream runs to
GG's, slow cruises down Bird River, campfires, deck time with your honey, and tanning on the dock. Warm,
sunny, singalong country — the kind of song that becomes a family's summer theme.*

> Keeps the personal fingerprints (Bird River, GG's) so it's *theirs*, while staying universal enough for
> any lake crowd to belt.

---

## Creative Direction

**Concept:** *Lake season as the good life — slow days, sunburns, and the people you love.* It's a list of
joys that builds into one big, repeatable hook ("lakeside"), with a tender bridge that zooms out: years
from now, this is still the place. Pure summer, pure family, pure singalong.

**Why it'll grip / sing-along:**
- **Concrete, vivid scenes** — turtles in the weeds, the screen-door slap, cannonball off the rock, tube
  wipeouts, GG's ice cream. Specifics make a country song feel real and fun.
- **One-word title hook** ("lakeside") + a wordless **"whoa-oh" post-chorus** = instant crowd participation.
- **Warm payoff bridge** keeps it from being just a party list — "some things money cannot buy and time
  cannot erase" gives it heart.

**Arc:** roll in, kids already at the water (V1) → the lake's calling (pre) → the big lakeside hook
(chorus) → dock + honey + tubing + campfire (V2) → no clocks, just this (pre 2) → years from now, still
here (bridge) → final chorus + chant.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** upbeat, sunny country summer anthem — radio-country, feel-good, singalong. Acoustic + bright
electric guitar, fiddle, banjo, pedal steel, hand-claps, live drums with a driving backbeat; big
gang-vocal chorus and a "whoa-oh" hook. Warm and organic, not slick.

- **Tempo:** ~**120–126 BPM** — bright, danceable, tailgate-on-the-dock energy.
- **Key:** **G major** (sunny), optional lift to **A** for the final chorus.
- **Vocal:** **male lead** — warm, easygoing, a little twang; conversational in the verses, big and open on
  the chorus. Stack gang vocals/harmonies on "lakeside" and the "whoa-oh" post-chorus. Clean, no autotune.

Gravel turns to cabin road, windows down and the radio loud,
