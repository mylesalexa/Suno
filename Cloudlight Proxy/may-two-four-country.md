# May Two-Four *(modern country-pop dance — Canadian long-weekend party anthem)*

*The coast-to-coast sibling to "Ten Bucks In (Going Social)" and "Hurry Hard" — same fusion, same
festival-swagger party energy, but built on the one thing every Canadian, not just the Prairies, relates
to instantly: the May long weekend. "Two-four" means the case of beer *and* the May 24th weekend, and
that pun carries the whole song — docks going back in, tarps coming off the boat, the first bonfire of the
year, hockey playoffs crackling on the radio. Classy, warm, a couple of well-placed "eh"s, never crude.
Clean. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Brief:** another one in the "social" party-anthem lane, but this time something *every* Canadian can
> relate to, not just a Prairie-specific tradition — kept classy, with a couple of "eh"s thrown in.

---

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** the May long weekend (Victoria Day, always around the 24th) is the unofficial start of
Canadian summer, coast to coast — cottages and camps reopen, docks go back in the water, boats come out of
storage, and everybody who's been buried in snow for six months finally cracks a case outside again.
**The pun that carries the whole song:** a "two-four" is a case of 24 beers *and* the date the long weekend
falls on — "May Two-Four" means both at once, and the chorus leans into that double meaning hard.
**Why it's universal, not regional:** unlike a wedding social or a bonspiel, this one isn't tied to any one
province — it's the Rockies, the Maritimes, Ontario cottage country, and the Prairie lake cabin all doing
the exact same thing on the exact same weekend.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** modern country-pop dance — four-on-the-floor, gritty electric guitar riff, banjo plucks over a
club beat, slap bass, stomp-clap groove, punchy live drums with an 808 layer, confident male lead, tight
harmony stacks, huge gang-vocal chorus, festival country swagger, polished radio mix. Same fusion lane as
its siblings, warm and communal rather than rowdy.

- **Tempo:** ~**128 BPM** (four-on-the-floor, same pocket as "Ten Bucks In").
- **Vocal:** confident **male lead** — tight harmony stacks, massive gang-vocal chorus.
