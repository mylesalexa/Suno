# Nets Up (Backyard Social) *(modern country-pop dance — sunset backyard party anthem)*

*Another sibling in the "Ten Bucks In" party-anthem family — same fusion, same festival-swagger energy —
built around a completely different Canadian-summer ritual: the backyard net game going in the grass, the
fire pit lit as the sun goes down, kids weaving around the flames while the adults play, and the whole
street ending up in one yard before anybody planned it. Clean. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Brief:** a new party-anthem sibling to the social song, built around a backyard scene — a net game
> going in the yard, sun setting, kids playing while a fire's going.

---

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** it's not a planned party — it's just a normal summer evening that turns into one. Somebody
stakes the net in the yard, somebody else lights the fire pit, and by sunset half the street's folding
chairs are out. Kids run circles around the fire while the grown-ups play the net game like it's the
Stanley Cup, and nobody ever actually decides to stay — the night just keeps going until the fire's down to
embers. **Why it works:** the details are specific and lived-in (staking the net in the grass, the cooler
lid popping, a dog stealing a hot dog off someone's plate, "one more game" every single time somebody tries
to leave) — the same kind of hyper-local realism that made the wedding-social song feel like a real place
instead of a generic party. **Why it's a fresh angle, not a reskin of "Ten Bucks In":** this one's built
around a repeating **list-style chorus** ("Nets up, fire's up, cooler's up too") instead of a chant line or
call-and-response, and it has its own rhythmic fingerprint — a **ball-bounce percussion accent** (the actual
thwack of the net game worked into the beat) the way broom-sweep percussion gave "Hurry Hard" its identity.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** modern country-pop dance — four-on-the-floor, gritty electric guitar riff, banjo plucks over a
club beat, slap bass, a **ball-bounce percussion accent** (a net-game thwack worked into the groove),
confident male lead, huge gang-vocal chorus, festival country swagger, polished mix. Warm and golden-hour
rather than rowdy-club — a backyard at sunset, not a rented hall at midnight.

- **Tempo:** ~**124 BPM** (four-on-the-floor, a touch warmer/slower than its siblings to match the sunset
  mood).
- **Vocal:** confident **male lead** — tight harmony stacks, huge gang-vocal chorus.
