# Steinbach Comedy — 4-Minute Stand-Up *(spoken word, NO music)*

*A genuinely authentic, belly-laugh Steinbach/Mennonite stand-up set — frugal, hard-working, judgey,
churchy, closet-drinking, deal-obsessed — written affectionately (Mennonites famously roast themselves),
punched up by a comedy panel, and split into two Suno generations because 4 minutes is past Suno's
~3,000-char (~3-min) lyric cap. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Suno can do this** (your test proved it — it spoke the roast, didn't sing it). 4 minutes won't fit one
> generation, so this is **Part 1 + Part 2** — generate each with the same style box, then stitch. Same
> "spoken word, no music, comedy club" prompt that worked.

## Research notes (a day's worth, distilled)
- **Frugality canon:** ice-cream pails = "Mennonite Tupperware" ("the pail's half the value"); clearance/
  garage-sale obsession; **Steinbach Credit Union** (one of Canada's biggest — they *save*); drive far to
  save a little. New Bothwell cheese nearby.
- **Food/faspa:** vareniki (+ schmaundtfat cream gravy), farmer sausage, rollkuchen with watermelon,
  borscht, zwieback buns; faspa = a feast called "a little something."
- **Faith/judgey:** 22+ churches; gossip reframed as "sharing concerns"; speed-limit minivans.
- **Closet drinkers:** dry town **until 2003**; "a Mennonite's dilemma = free beer"; the classic *"take two
  Mennonites fishing"* logic.
- **Hard-working:** retirement isn't a concept; 80-somethings still on the roof.
- **Tone:** Mennonites embrace self-mockery — keep it *with* them, no profanity, no faith-bashing.

```
(applause, crowd settles)
Steinbach, Manitoba! The only city in Canada where the bank is bigger than the bar.
(laughter)
Steinbach Credit Union — these folks don't spend money, they gather it and admire it. A Mennonite doesn't have a savings account; he has a calling.
(laughter, applause)

You know you're in a Mennonite kitchen when the freezer is full of containers and not one of them tells the truth. The tub says "ice cream" — that's chili. The margarine — also chili.
(laughter)
There's a yogurt container in there older than I am, three labels deep, every one crossed out in marker. That pail has outlived two freezers and a marriage.
(laughter)
You do not throw out a good ice-cream pail in this town. You wash it, you stack it, and one day you leave it to your kids in the will.
(laughter, applause)

A Mennonite cannot physically walk past a deal. Put a yellow clearance sticker on a brick, he'll buy two. "When am I gonna need a brick?" Doesn't matter — it was eighty percent off.
(laughter)
I watched a man drive forty-five minutes to New Bothwell to save three dollars on cheese, burn nine in gas, and call it the best day of his life.
(laughter)
Garage sale opens at eight? There's a Friesen in the driveway at six forty-five. Cash. And a strategy.
(laughter, applause)

And they never stop working. Retirement is not a Mennonite concept.
(laughter)
I saw an eighty-four-year-old man shingling a roof in February. I said, "Sir, be careful!" He said, "Ach — I'll rest when I'm dead."
(laughter)
Then he climbed down and kept building his own coffin. To save on the funeral.
(big laughter, applause)
```

```
Then there's faspa. Faspa is when a Mennonite serves you a four-course meal and calls it "a little something."

There's enough food to end a famine and she is genuinely hurt you can't manage a seventh sausage. You don't leave a Mennonite house hungry — you leave needing a nap and a bigger belt.

Twenty-two churches in one town. Sunday morning it's a NASCAR of minivans — all doing exactly the speed limit, because Jesus is watching... and so is Mrs. Penner.
(laughter)
Mennonites don't gossip. They "lift each other up in conversation." "Now, I'm not one to judge, but did you SEE who was at the bar Friday?" That's not concern, Brenda. That's surveillance.

And if they really don't like you, they add you to the prayer list — by full name — so the whole town knows what you did and that God's been notified.
(big laughter, applause)

Now — officially — Steinbach was dry until 2003. Officially.
(laughter)
But drive to the off-sale one town over and read the plates. All Steinbach. All parked nose-out — for a quick getaway.
(laughter)
A Mennonite doesn't drink. He "samples." He "has a little something for the cold." It's July.
(laughter)
Every basement's got a homemade wine that started as grape juice and found religion — forty percent, blessed personally. And he'll never buy just one beer, that looks like a habit. He buys a flat. For "guests." The guests are him.
(big laughter, applause)

Last thing — you cannot date in this town, because everybody's a cousin. Six last names, forty thousand people. You meet a Friesen, you don't ask IF you're related — you ask how closely.
(laughter)
But honestly? I love these people. Hardest-working, biggest-hearted, cheapest, holiest, sneakiest-drinking folks on Earth — and they will give you the shirt off their back.
(beat)
It's a clearance shirt. But still.
(laughter)
Steinbach, you've been incredible — drive safe, tithe generously, and somebody tell Mrs. Penner I went home early. Goodnight!
```

## Notes & tips
- **Why two parts:** ~3,000 chars ≈ ~3 min in Suno; 4 min needs Part 1 + Part 2, stitched. Each part above
  is its own generation with the **same** style/exclude boxes.
- **Keep it talking, not singing:** lead with `Stand-up comedy, spoken word, no music`; keep
  `singing, melody, music` excluded; if a take drifts musical, add `(deadpan spoken delivery)` and
  regenerate (it took to spoken word last time, so you're in good shape).
- **The big laughs** (panel-tested): the ice-cream-pail "investment," the coffin topper, the "surveillance"
  line, and the **closet-drinker bit** ("the guests are him") — protect those, they're the roof-raisers.
- **Edge stays kind:** no profanity, no faith-bashing; it lands *because* it ends on love (the clearance-
  shirt callback). That's the affectionate-roast sweet spot Mennonites actually enjoy.
