# Steinbach Roast — Stand-Up Comedy *(spoken word, NO music — a Suno stress-test)*

*A ~3-minute affectionate-but-edgy stand-up roast of Steinbach / Mennonite culture, written as a
spoken-word Suno prompt (no singing, no instruments) to test whether Suno can do comedy. Clean (no
profanity), warm at the core. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Can Suno actually do this?** Sort of — Suno is built for *music*, so spoken comedy is its weakest event.
> It can produce spoken-word + crowd-laughter ambiance, but it often tries to add melody/cadence or "sing"
> the lines. Expect to **generate several times** and pick the best, and don't be shocked if it leans
> musical. The prompt below is tuned to fight that (heavy "spoken word, no music, comedy club" + a strong
> Exclude list). Honest verdict: usable for a comedy *bit/ambiance*, not a guaranteed clean stand-up set.

---

## Research notes (so the roast lands true)
Steinbach, MB — founded **1874** by Plautdietsch (Low German) Mennonites; **22+ churches** (≈1 per 820
people); nicknamed **"The Automobile City"** (J.R. Friesen); **dry town until 2003**; faspa, farmer
sausage, vereniki, MCC Thrift, the surname pool (Friesen/Penner/Reimer/Wiebe/Loewen/Toews), the "Mennonite
game" (figuring out how you're related), thrift, big trucks/minivans. Roast is *with* the town, not at it.

## ⬇️ Ready to Prompt Suno (Custom Mode)

**Title (≤80 chars)**
```
Steinbach Roast (Stand-Up)
```

**Style of Music box (paste exactly)**
```
Stand-up comedy, spoken word, no music, live comedy club, single male comedian, dry witty conversational delivery, audience laughter and applause, mic, no instruments, no melody
```

**Exclude Styles box (paste exactly)**
```
music, singing, melody, instruments, beat, rap, autotune, background music, jingle, score
```

**Lyrics box (the set — paste exactly)**
```
[Spoken word, stand-up comedy, no music, comedy club, mic]

(applause, crowd settles)
Steinbach, Manitoba! Give it up!
(cheers)
You can clap — it's allowed now. Took this town till 2003 to legally buy a drink, but clapping's been fine since at least the nineties.
(laughter)

A whole city that looked at alcohol and went, "Na, ohba... we'll just bottle our judgment instead."
(big laughter)
You couldn't buy a beer here, but you could buy a Ford. They call it "The Automobile City." Priorities, folks — can't have a drink, but you CAN have a three-quarter-ton truck to haul your feelings around in.
(laughter, applause)

Twenty-two churches. Twenty-two! One house of worship for every eight hundred people. That's not a town, that's a spiritual timeshare.
(laughter)
Sunday morning, the traffic's worse than Winnipeg — bumper to bumper minivans, every single one doing exactly the speed limit. Because speeding is a sin... and so is being late for faspa.
(laughter)

Faspa! For the English in the room — that's when a Mennonite eats a full meal and calls it a snack. Buns, cheese, cold ham, four kinds of jam, and coffee strong enough to baptize you.
(laughter)
Grandma puts out enough food to feed a Russian village — which, historically, checks out — then says, "Ach, I didn't make much."
(laughter)
You leave four thousand calories heavier and she's hurt you didn't have thirds.
(laughter, applause)

And the names. Friesen, Penner, Reimer, Wiebe, Loewen, Toews. Six last names for forty thousand people.
(laughter)
You meet a Friesen, you don't ask IF you're related — you ask HOW. Everybody's somebody's cousin, which really tightens up the dating pool. That's why they invented the minivan — you gotta drive to Winnipeg to meet someone you're NOT related to.
(big laughter, applause)

These are the most efficient people on Earth. A Mennonite will drive forty minutes to save two bucks on cheese and call it a miracle.
(laughter)
And the Low German — Plautdietsch — beautiful language. Sounds like German and Dutch got in an argument in a barn.
(laughter)
Half of it is just them complaining quietly so the English don't catch on. "Na jo... ohba..." That's not weather talk, folks. That's them roasting YOU.
(laughter, applause)

Real talk before I go — I didn't even write this. I typed "roast Steinbach" into an app called Suno and it spat out the whole set.
(laughter)
An AI already knows you people. Be afraid.
(big laughter)
Steinbach — you've been beautiful! Drive home safe, do the speed limit, and tell your cousin I said hi. Goodnight!
(applause, cheering)
```

## Notes & tips
- **Promoting/"prompting" Suno:** the closer has a built-in **Suno plug** (the "I typed it into Suno" bit).
  Keep it for the meta-joke, or delete that block if you want a pure roast.
- **Getting Suno to talk, not sing:** lead the style box with `Stand-up comedy, spoken word, no music`;
  keep `singing, melody, music, instruments` in Exclude; if it still sings, add `(deadpan spoken delivery)`
  and try lowering the style-influence/weirdness, and regenerate a few times.
- **Length:** ~3 minutes as written (≈480 words). If a take rushes or cuts off, split it into two
  generations (first half / second half) and stitch them.
- **Crowd laughs:** the `(laughter)` / `(applause)` tags cue Suno to leave gaps and add ambiance — they
  also just read like a real set. Remove them if Suno tries to "perform" the word "laughter."
- **Keep it kind:** it's a *with-you* roast (thrift, faspa, dry-town, big trucks) — no profanity, no
  attacking anyone's faith. Edgy via affection, which is why it works.
