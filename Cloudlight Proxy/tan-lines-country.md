# Tan Lines *(the one that turned into forever)*

*The chosen summer single — upgraded. Starts as a no-strings summer fling both people swore was temporary
("just tan lines come September"), then **unexpectedly becomes the love of a lifetime.** The hook flips
from a fading memento into a forever: from "all I'd have left was tan lines" to "give me a lifetime of tan
lines." Feel-good summer country with a groove, warm male lead. Cloudlight Proxy.*

---

## The arc (why it's better)
- **Verse 1:** meet at the boat launch, both swear it's nothing — "you'll be gone like a sunburn."
- **Chorus (early):** the *expectation* — come fall, all I'll have left is the tan lines.
- **Verse 2 + Pre:** it doesn't end. Maybe turns into "meet my folks." The turn: *neither one of us left.*
- **Bridge:** the unexpected forever — a ring on the same dock, white dress, whole town.
- **Final Chorus (flipped):** decades later, still going back every June — "give me a lifetime of tan lines."

The genius of the flip: the listener thinks they're getting a flirty fling song, and it quietly becomes
the song you play at the wedding *and* the 40th anniversary — that's what makes it a keep-forever repeat.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** feel-good summer country with a pocket groove — flirty and breezy in the verses, warm and full
on the choruses, a swelling bridge for the forever-turn. Acoustic-driven, bright electric, fiddle, claps.

- **Tempo:** ~**112 BPM** — flirty mid-uptempo groove; let the final chorus open up biggest.
- **Key:** **D major**, lift to **E** for the final chorus.
- **Vocal:** **male lead** — warm, easygoing, a grin in the verses that turns sincere by the bridge.
  Stacked harmonies + claps on the hook. Clean, no autotune. *(Female-lead swap noted at the bottom.)*
