# True North, Eh *(a Canadian identity anthem)*

*A sweeping, proud honky-tonk country anthem about what it actually means to be Canadian — the Rockies,
the curling rinks, the white Christmases, the bone-cold winters, and the unwritten rule that you show up
for your neighbour without being asked. Researched in the lane of **Dean Brody's "Canadian Girls"** — a
#1 Canadian country-radio anthem built on warm twang, patriotic pride, and big singalong energy — but
repointed from a tribute-to-women song into a tribute-to-a-country song. Has a distinctly conservative
heartbeat: Canada is warm and welcoming, but it has a way of doing things, and if you're coming here,
you're expected to bring your whole self to it. Clean. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Brief:** research "Canadian Girls" and write a true national-identity anthem — not just the curling
> song (that's already written), but a full-scope Canadian pride song covering the Rockies, curling, "eh,"
> the winters, white Christmases, the mutual-aid culture, and a firm-but-warm message: if you come to
> Canada, be prepared to fit in. Conservative vibe. Honky-tonk anthem lane.

## Researching the style

- **"Canadian Girls" (Dean Brody)** → honky-tonk country, #1 on Canadian country radio, written explicitly
  as a patriotic tribute. Warm twang, fiddle, big singalong chorus — Canada's answer to "California Girls."
  Pride without preachiness. The reference lane this whole song family lives in.
- **"Canadian Railroad Trilogy" (Gordon Lightfoot) / Stompin' Tom Connors** → the tradition of songs that
  name the actual geography (Rockies, prairies, Maritimes) and make the land itself feel heroic. Tapped
  here for the *scope* — the lyric should make you see the whole country, not just one region.
- **What makes this different from "Curling Night" (already in this catalog):** "Curling Night" is
  about one sport as a shared pastime — honky-tonk anthem, warm, proud, club-focused. "True North, Eh" is
  about Canadian *identity* — wider scope, includes the values message ("fit in"), the Christmas imagery,
  the mutual-aid ethic, and the Rockies. Different emotional register: not just proud, but *declarative.*

---

## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** Canada isn't just a country on a map — it's a set of conditions and a code. The Rockies
standing over you. Winters that don't apologize. White Christmases every year going back to your
grandparents. A curling rink in every little town, lit up on a Friday night. And the unwritten rule that
when your neighbour's truck is stuck, you don't wait to be invited — you grab the shovel. That's the song.
The "fit in" message isn't hostile: it's the natural extension of that code. We'll help you through anything
— but you carry your weight. That's not a warning, that's an invitation to belong to something real.

**Why the "eh" matters:** it's not a punchline here. "Eh" is the sound of a country that checks in with
you — it's inclusive by reflex. It ends the chorus not as a joke but as a signature. *Welcome to Canada,
we're glad you're here, eh* — that little word is doing warmth and identity at the same time.

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** honky-tonk country anthem — the same lane as "Curling Night" but slightly more declarative,
slightly less wistful. Fiddle, pedal steel, acoustic and electric guitar, warm country production.
Proud, patriotic, singalong. No club beat, no 808, no dance-floor drop. Built to be sung standing up.

- **Tempo:** ~**108 BPM** (big singalong stride — sturdier than a two-step, not a race).
- **Key:** **A major** (bright, declarative, anthem-forward).
- **Vocal:** warm, firm **male lead** — confident delivery, not aggressive. Full harmonies on the chorus.
  The kind of voice that sounds right next to a campfire and a flag at the same time.
