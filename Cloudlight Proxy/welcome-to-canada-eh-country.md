# Welcome to Canada, Eh *(warm conservative Canadian country anthem)*

*A proud, warm, whole-crowd **Canadian country festival anthem** — the Rockies, curling, Tim Hortons,
white Christmases, "eh," helping your neighbour, and a clear message: this country was built on something
real, and if you come here, you honour that and fit right in. Welcoming but unapologetic. Big singalong
chorus, fiddle, the works. Clean. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Sound target:** the same warm, polished **radio/arena country festival anthem** voice as "Everybody
> Knows Somebody" / "Back Where We Belong" — powerful clean male lead, tight harmonies, whole-crowd chorus.
> Proud and communal, not angry. The message is clear but the door is open. Original lyrics; no artist
> names in the Suno box.

---

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** a love letter to Canada that doubles as a confident "here's who we are" — the cold, the curling,
the Timmies, the neighbours helping neighbours, the white Christmas, the Rockies. The chorus is the welcome
mat: come on in, we're glad you're here — *but know what you're stepping into.* We built this true north
on honest work and real values, and we're not shy about it.
**Conservative angle:** pride in tradition, community self-reliance (we help each other, not because we have
to — because we choose to), national identity without apology, the flag means something. Welcoming + firm.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** warm, anthemic **modern country** — radio/arena country, acoustic + electric guitars, fiddle,
driving drums, big singalong chorus with tight harmonies and gang backing vocals. Polished, sunny, proud.

- **Tempo:** ~**128–132 BPM** — upbeat, driving, roomy enough for a whole crowd to sing.
- **Key:** **G major** (bright, warm, big arena lift on the chorus).
- **Vocal:** **powerful, warm, polished male lead** — clean and strong, big and emotive on the chorus, with
  **tight harmonies + crowd/gang vocals** on the hook. Clean.

## ⭐ Best Suno prompt
```
Warm anthemic modern country, 130 BPM, key of G, acoustic and electric guitars, fiddle, driving drums, powerful male lead, tight harmonies, huge singalong chorus, radio arena country
```
**Tip:** the anthem magic is **warm + proud + communal** — `powerful male lead`, `tight harmonies`, and
`fiddle` up front for the Canadian warmth. Keep the chorus `big singalong` with `crowd/gang vocals`. `dark,
grunge, sad ballad` in Exclude keeps it sunny and uplifting. If it drifts soft, add `driving drums, arena
country`; if too rock, add `acoustic guitar, radio country mix, pedal steel`.

## Notes
- **"True north strong and free"** is from O Canada — perfectly on-purpose, the whole crowd knows it.
- **The message is warm, not hostile:** "hold the door, pour the coffee, give you half of what we've got"
  before the "fit right in" line — the welcome is genuine; the values are just non-negotiable.
- The **Bridge** is the boldest moment: "there's a flag on every porch that means something around here" —
  swap to "there's a reason that this flag flies proud around here" if you want it a touch softer.

## Variations
- **French Canada nod:** add a single French line in the bridge ("vive le nord — this is our home") for
  national unity without losing the conservative tone.
- **Bigger crowd closing:** tag `(crowd shouts) EH!` after every chorus line for the full festival call-back.
- **Slower, more solemn cut:** ~104 BPM, `acoustic ballad, heartfelt, intimate` for a Remembrance Day /
  heritage feel instead of a festival banger.
