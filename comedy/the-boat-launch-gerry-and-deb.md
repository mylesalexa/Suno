# The Boat Launch (Gerry & Deb) *(comedy skit-song — married-couple boat launch disaster)*

*A comedy skit set to music about the single most stressful ten minutes of cottage life: backing the Grand
Caravan down the ramp to launch the pontoon while half the marina watches. **Polished cut:** the story is
now carried by one narrator — Gerry — with Deb's voice cutting in as short, sharp one-liners rather than
full alternating verses, so it reads as one coherent comedic story instead of a back-and-forth transcript.
The two voices come fully together for exactly one moment — the funniest one — plus the spoken intro,
skit break, and outro. Built in the **Bob & Doug McKenzie / Great White North** lane (deadpan Canadian
sketch-comedy banter set to a goofy rock song) crossed with **Wayne's World**-style "we are not worthy"
absurdist escalation. Clean, loud, dumb on purpose. Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Brief:** new comedy folder. A parody skit-song about the boat launch and all its failures — the Grand
> Caravan trying to pull the pontoon, the two of them arguing the whole time. Dig deep on the humor.
> Reference lane: Wayne's World stupid, Bob & Doug McKenzie's *Twelve Days of Christmas* / "Take Off"
> energy. **Revision note:** the original draft had Gerry and Deb fully alternating every section, which
> read oddly close together — polished so Gerry narrates the story straight through, Deb interjects

## Researching the lane
*(artist/character names are filtered in Suno, so these become sound descriptors, not names)*
- **Bob & Doug McKenzie's "Great White North" sketches** → deadpan, rambling **Canadian duo banter** set
  loosely to music — mundane disaster narrated calmly, "eh"s and hoser vernacular, warmth underneath the
  absurdity. Their song *"Take Off"* is a goofy mid-tempo novelty-rock single with a big dumb singalong
  hook and spoken-word bits woven through the verses.
- **Wayne's World** → **absurdist escalation** — small problems spiral into full chaos while everyone
  stays weirdly calm about it, played completely straight-faced, which is what makes it funnier.
- **The fusion, plainly:** a goofy, twangy **skit-rock** backing track, spoken comedy dialogue between two
  characters (Gerry and Deb) woven in and out of sung verses, a big dumb crowd-chant chorus, and a real
  physical-comedy plot (the trailer, the ramp, the honking, the van rolling into the lake) instead of just
  a vibe.

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** Gerry is *supremely* confident backing a boat trailer down a boat launch. He is bad at it,
and he's the one telling you the story — narrating the whole disaster in first person, a little too proud
of himself the entire time. Deb doesn't narrate; she interrupts, with short, dry one-liners that puncture
whatever Gerry just claimed. The pontoon jackknifes, the line of trucks behind them starts honking, the van
ends up with its back tires in the lake, and by the end they're both laughing about it and doing it again
next year anyway. **The comedy engine:** real, specific boat-launch chaos (trailer jackknife, left/right
confusion, forgetting park for reverse, an audience of strangers), one confident unreliable narrator, and
one dry voice keeping him honest — played with total sincerity, nobody breaking character to acknowledge
how funny it is.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** **novelty comedy skit-rock** — twangy classic-rock guitar, playful bouncing bass, a goofy
harmonica line, brushed-to-driving drums depending on the chaos level. **Spoken-word comedy dialogue**
woven between sung sections, sound effects (honking, splashing, a barking dog, a crowd) throughout, big
gang-vocal chorus. Clean, loud, deliberately dumb, warm underneath.

- **Tempo:** ~**108 BPM** (bouncy mid-tempo classic-rock groove; can push faster during the chaos peak).
- **Key:** **G major** (bright, simple, built for a dumb singalong).
- **Vocal:** **primary male narrator (Gerry)** — confident, slightly-too-proud, carries both verses
  start to finish — with a **dry female voice (Deb)** cutting in as short one-line interjections, not full
  alternating verses. Both voices join in unison/harmony on the chorus, and fully duet just once, at the
  comedic peak (the "park vs. reverse" bridge). Clean, comedic, sincere delivery — never winking at the camera.

## Variations
- **Even more one-sided:** cut Deb's interjections down to just the Bridge duet and the spoken outro, making Gerry's narration the only sung voice on the entire track.
- **Bigger chaos ending:** add `everyone in the parking lot cheering, kazoo solo` for an even sillier finish.
- **More Bob & Doug lane:** add `rambling comedic narration, dry Canadian delivery, toque and back-porch energy`.
- **More Wayne's World lane:** add `mock-triumphant, air guitar moment, over-the-top sincerity`.
- **Radio edit:** trim the intro and outro skits down to one exchange each for a tighter, more music-forward cut.
- **Title/hook swaps:** "Backin' 'Er In," "Adjacent to the Water," "Same Time Next Year," "Cut the Wheel."
