## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** "Clean life" gets three meanings at once, which is what keeps it fun for kids: **clean as in tidy** (made my bed, room's picked up), **clean as in kind and good-hearted** (kind words online, nobody left out of the group chat), and **clean as in the planet** (recycled the bottles). All of it adds up to the "dream life" — the idea that the dream is built from small good daily choices, not from a crown or fancy stuff.

**"This day and age" is baked into the details:** screen time kept in check, headphones, a group chat where nobody's left out, kind words "when I type or I speak," and "good habits daily — that's the streak" (a nod to the habit-streak counters kids know from apps and games). The song never preaches against technology; it just shows a kid who uses it well and still goes outside.

**Four things that make it work for kids and parents:**
1. **The hook is a chantable phrase kids already say:** "keeping it clean" with a real, positive meaning.
2. **Concrete, relatable actions instead of vague "be good" advice** — made my bed, held the doors, finished my book, full water bottle.
3. **The bridge is the emotional peak:** "You don't need a crown to be a star / Just a good heart and who you are" — a line a parent will be happy to hear on repeat.
4. **The stamp is two words a kid can shout:** "Dream big!"

**Repetition kept limited:** the hook phrase ("clean life") shows up in the chorus and post-chorus without being hammered, the chorus is 6 lines, and the post-chorus is 3.

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** **motivational pop-trap** — punchy 808 bass, bright synth lead, crisp claps, melodic rap verses that open into a big sing-along hook. Energetic and uplifting, with all of the aggression of the reference sound removed.

- **Tempo:** **86 BPM** — half-time trap pulse that still feels driving, with hats doubling up on the final chorus.
- **Key:** **A major** (relative major of the reference's F♯ minor).
- **Vocal:** confident, upbeat **male lead** — melodic rap on the verses, singing on the hook, group sing-along from the first chorus.

**Tip:** `punchy 808 bass` and `bright synth lead` are the two ingredients that carry the motivational-trap sound; keep both. `kid-friendly, positive` in the style box plus `scary, dark, aggressive` in Exclude is what keeps Suno from drifting toward the heavier mood of the reference sound.

- Too dark or serious → add `bright, uplifting, playful`; keep `dark, scary` in Exclude
- Verses too fast for kids to follow → pull to 82 BPM; add `clear enunciation, simple flow`
- Hook not sing-along enough → add `chantable chorus, group vocals, call and response`
- Beat too heavy → drop `808` emphasis; add `light bounce, bright claps`
- More toddler-friendly → swap `melodic rap verses` for `sung verses`, add `nursery-rhyme simple`

## Variations
- **Female lead:** swap `male lead` → `female lead` for an equally strong take.
- **Younger kids (ages 3-6):** pull to 100 BPM, add `playful, bouncy, sung verses`, and drop the "streak" and "screen time" lines.
- **Classroom version:** add a spoken "Ready? Let's go!" intro and a clap-along break before the final chorus.
- **Acoustic / family sing-along:** `acoustic guitar, hand claps, warm, kids choir` — same lyrics, campfire-style.
- **Title/hook swaps:** "Living the Dream," "Keeping It Clean," "Dream Team," "Rise and Shine."
