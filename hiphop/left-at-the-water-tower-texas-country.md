## Research — What the Hit Actually Does

Only one of the three sources I tried loaded fully (Billboard redirected to another host and Texas Monthly returned a 403), and part of that page was a machine-generated summary I ignored. What the readable part of the Wikipedia article supports:

- **A clever reversal built on a made-up phrase:** the song grew from a phrase Langley invented after a story from co-writer Miranda Lambert; the twist is that the "other woman" is a **place**, not a person. ([Wikipedia: Choosin' Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choosin%27_Texas))
- **A chorus that lists evidence:** it's built from the obvious signs she missed, with specific references to songs he loved. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choosin%27_Texas))
- **Lyrics that sting over a groove that swings:** one critic (Holler, quoted on that page) says the melody swings like a line dance while the lyrics sting. Billboard's Jessica Nicholson credits the production, rhythm, message and voice together. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choosin%27_Texas))
- **Real guitar playing:** the credits list guitar including steel guitar, played by session guitarists, with Miranda Lambert on background vocals, recorded in Nashville. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choosin%27_Texas))

**Not given by any page I read:** tempo, key, groove details, vocal approach or mix. So my 100 BPM and A major are choices, not facts.

### What I took from it (and didn't)

| What makes the hit work | How this song tries to do the same thing |
|---|---|
| **A twist in the hook:** the rival is a place | **The route out of town is the hook**, and by the end every road on it passes a memory ("Every road out of here runs right through us") |
| **The chorus lists the signs** | **The chorus lists directions** with landmarks; verse 2 lists the signs she missed ("read maps like love letters") |
| **Lyrics sting, melody swings** | Sass on top ("Bless your heart — go on"), a danceable two-step underneath, a catch in the voice |
| **Specific, familiar references** | Dairy Queen, a cattle-country water tower, a screen door in a tornado, pie "on the house," a man named Earl; **no artist names**, since Suno filters them |
| **A guitar hook worth humming** | A twangy guitar lick opens the song, leads each chorus and gets its own break |

**What I deliberately didn't borrow:** the heartbreak-over-a-state premise, the song references, and anything from the lyrics. The story here is a different one (she's giving him directions to leave).

## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** The sass is the cover story. She keeps it light: sweet tea for the drive, "Bless your heart," a pie from the waitress, a Friday two-step with an eighty-two-year-old named Earl. But every direction she gives him passes a place they were, and the porch light is still on. The bridge is where the sass drops and the song admits it.

**Four lines doing the heavy lifting:**
1. **"Smiled like it didn't cost me"** (verse 1) is the thesis, understated.
2. **"Fine as a screen door in a tornado"** is the honky-tonk wit.
3. **"Fine as a porch light I can't turn off"** is the ache hidden in the joke.
4. **"Every road out of here runs right through us"** is the sting.

**Clean:** no profanity; "Bless your heart" and "hon" are the sharpest things in it.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** **70s-80s Texas honky-tonk country-pop**: a swaying two-step, a twangy electric guitar hook, pedal steel, rolling bass and live drums.

- **Tempo:** **100 BPM** (my choice).
- **Key:** **A major**; the final chorus lifts a step.
- **Vocal:** **smoky Southern drawl female lead**: conversational and sassy on the verses, a little rough around the edges, and soft and honest in the bridge. Gang vocals only at the start of the final chorus.

## How to give it the best shot against a real record

- **Generate many takes.** Each run gives you more than one version; run it five to ten times and keep the best, because voice quality varies a lot between renders.
- **The vocal is what you'll hear first.** If it sounds too polished or too young, add `husky, raspy, lived-in, natural phrasing` and remove `sassy`. If it sounds like a pop singer, add `traditional country vocalist`.
- **Protect the guitar hook.** If the lick is buried, add `prominent melodic guitar lick` (drop `bittersweet` to stay under 200 characters).
- **Keep it organic.** `auto-tune`, `electronic beats` and `heavy distortion` are already excluded; add `organic, live band, vintage` if it still sounds modern.
- **Fix one section at a time.** If a take is great except the chorus, use Suno's extend/replace-section tools instead of throwing the take away (the exact controls depend on your Suno version).
- **Compare blind.** Play your best take back-to-back with the record and note what's missing, then tell me and I'll adjust the prompt or words.
