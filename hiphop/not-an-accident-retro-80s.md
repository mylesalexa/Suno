# Not an Accident *(retro 80s soft-pop meets guitar-riff rock — "no coincidences" anthem)*

*One-line concept: a stranger says the exact thing you needed to hear and vanishes before you can say thanks — and that's the whole argument for the song: nothing lines up like that by accident, and neither do you.*

*A hybrid of the two 80s lanes the catalog has now built: the "First Time in San Juan" warmth of the soft-pop tracks, merged with a genuine electric guitar riff hook (toned down from the full arena-rock scale of "Strangers 'Til Sunrise" — one catchy riff, not a wall of dual-harmonized guitars). Deep theme, fun delivery — this is meant to feel like a party, not a sermon.*

---

## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** The premise is Hebrews 13:2's old idea — "some have entertained angels without knowing it" — turned into a genuinely fun pop song instead of a hymn. Verse 1 sets up the mystery: a stranger shows up at exactly the right moment with exactly the right words, then disappears before you can even get a name. Verse 2 widens it — the old friend you ran into twice in one week, the door that closed so a better one could open, the flight you almost missed that seated you next to the exact right person. The chorus makes the turn explicit and personal: **you're not an accident either.** The theme (you were made on purpose, for a purpose) is the same weight as "Leave It Better," but the delivery here is meant to be danceable and a little playful — a wink, not a lecture.

**Why the guitar riff matters here specifically:** the user's own read on this catalog's last rock pivot ("Strangers 'Til Sunrise") was that a full arena-rock wall was too big a swerve — but the *riff itself* was the part that landed. This song is the merge: keep the San Juan-lane electric piano and group warmth as the bed, but let one bright, catchy electric guitar riff be the signature hook that opens the song, drives the chorus, and gets a real feature moment in the bridge — a hybrid instead of a full genre swap.

**Four things that keep this fun instead of preachy:**
1. **The hook opens with a shrug, not a sermon:** "Call it a coincidence — I don't." Contrarian and funny, not declarative.
2. **The verses are anecdotes, not arguments** — a gas station, a missed flight, an old friend twice in one week. Specific and a little funny ("Someone's the arranger" as the verse 2 punchline) rather than solemn.
3. **The bridge flips the idea on the listener with a wink:** "Maybe you've talked to one and didn't know / Maybe you've been one, and never will know" — turns the whole song back on the audience playfully instead of preaching at them.
4. **The stamp is two words and a shout, not a scripture reference:** "On purpose!" — a party chant that happens to carry real weight, same trick the whole catalog has been using since "No plans!"

**Why it's distinct from "Leave It Better":**
- That song is a slow, reflective swell (104 BPM, quiet conviction). This one is upbeat and danceable (112 BPM, guitar riff driving the chorus) — deep theme, fun tempo.
- That song's thesis is about what YOU leave behind. This one's thesis is about the fact that YOU were left behind on purpose — a companion idea, told from the other direction.
- New sonic signature: this is the first song in the retro lane to feature a real guitar riff as a recurring hook element rather than the electric piano carrying everything alone.

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** **retro 80s soft-pop / yacht rock, hybridized with a catchy electric guitar riff hook** — warm electric piano and synth pads still form the bed, but a bright, clean guitar riff opens the song, drives the chorus, and gets a featured moment in the bridge. Driving drums keep it upbeat and fun rather than reflective.

- **Tempo:** **112 BPM** — upbeat and danceable, closer to the party-lane songs than "Leave It Better"'s reflective pace. This is meant to move.
- **Key:** **G major**; lifts to **A major** for the final chorus, guitar riff soaring on the lift.
- **Vocal:** warm, playful **male lead** — full of genuine wonder on the verses, big and fun on the chorus. Group singalong enters by the first chorus; the post-chorus stamp is shouted with joy, not solemnity.
