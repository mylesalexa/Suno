# One More Thing *(old-school storytelling rap — "Fresh Prince" theme homage — renovation comedy)*

*One-line concept: the house was one weekend from finished. Then the wife opened forty Pinterest tabs, and by Tuesday there's a wall down that wasn't supposed to come down, over a paint color that looks exactly like the last eleven paint colors.*

*A structural homage to the Fresh Prince of Bel-Air theme — the laid-back, conversational narrative-rap frame ("let me tell you a story about how...") and the classic early-90s boom bap sitcom-theme sound (funky bassline, scratches, bright horns), applied to a scope-creep renovation story instead of a move to Bel-Air. Original lyrics throughout — the homage is in the storytelling structure and production style, not in reused lines.*

---

## Research — Grounding the Homage and the Jokes

- **The actual Fresh Prince of Bel-Air theme** ("Yo Home to Bel-Air") runs about **100-101 BPM**, was written by Will Smith and produced by DJ Jazzy Jeff, and is built as a first-person, conversational narrative rap — a story told in order, verse by verse, escalating toward a punchline ending. That tempo and storytelling structure is the direct template here. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fresh_Prince_of_Bel-Air_theme_song) · [SongBPM](https://songbpm.com/@dj-jazzy-jeff-the-fresh-prince/the-fresh-prince-of-bel-air))
- **"Scope creep" is the actual industry term** for exactly this phenomenon — a renovation project quietly expanding past its original plan because "while we're at it, we might as well..." keeps happening. It's a real, widely-documented pattern, not an exaggeration invented for the song. ([Corinthian Fine Homes](https://corinthianinc.com/scope-creep-on-your-home-remodeling-project-what-to-do-about-it/))
- **Paint color indecisiveness is a genuinely universal renovation experience** — one real account describes aiming for "a light yellow, a subtle look" and ending up with neon lime green; the broader research consistently flags paint/flooring/layout choices as the single most drawn-out, draining decision point in any renovation. That's the real basis for the "forty-seven shades of white" bit — it's barely an exaggeration. ([Remodelaholic](https://www.remodelaholic.com/worst-things-about-home-renovations/))

## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** The story follows the classic Fresh Prince arc — a normal life, a sudden disruption, and an escalating chain of events that end somewhere nobody planned. Here, "somewhere nobody planned" is a torn-out kitchen island and a fourth round of the same white paint. The chorus hook, "just one more thing," is the actual renovation experience turned into a singalong — every homeowner who's lived through a "quick" project knows exactly what that phrase means and exactly how it ends.

**Four things that make this land as funny instead of just a complaint:**
1. **The hook is universally recognizable dread** — "just one more thing" doesn't need explaining to anyone who's ever renovated anything.
2. **The paint color bit is the comedic centerpiece because it's true:** naming real-sounding paint names ("Alabaster," "Swiss Coffee," "Simply White") back to back is funnier than inventing fake ones, because everyone who's stood in a paint aisle recognizes the exact specific insanity of it.
3. **The bridge is a mini standup bit:** just reading paint names in a deadpan, increasingly exasperated voice ("Eggshell. Oyster. Bone. Dove. Cloud.") before landing on "they're all just white" is a complete joke on its own.
4. **The ending doesn't resolve — it escalates,** which is both truer to real renovations and a better joke than a tidy "and then it was finished" ending.

**Why the Fresh Prince frame specifically:** the source song's whole structure is "let me tell you how a small thing turned my whole life upside down" — that's *exactly* the shape of a renovation scope-creep story, just at a much smaller and funnier scale (a Pinterest board instead of a street fight). The homage isn't just a style choice, it's the right narrative container for this specific story.

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** **old-school boom bap hip-hop, storytelling rap** — a funky, sample-based bassline, DJ scratches, bright horn stabs, boom bap drums. Sitcom-theme-song energy: bouncy, warm, immediately recognizable as a "story rap" rather than a battle rap.

- **Tempo:** **100 BPM** — matches the real Fresh Prince theme's tempo, laid-back enough to carry conversational storytelling.
- **Key/feel:** bright and playful, not aggressive — comedic warmth over edge.
- **Vocal:** conversational, laid-back **male storytelling rap lead** — more Will Smith than Eminem, easygoing delivery that lets the punchlines land without rushing them. Chorus opens up into a full group singalong.

**Tip:** `funky bassline` + `DJ scratches` + `bright horn stabs` are the three ingredients that make this read as "sitcom theme" rather than generic old-school rap — don't drop any of them. - Not funky enough → add `slap bass, wah guitar, 90s sample sound`
- Missing the sitcom-theme feel → add `bright brass hits, theme song energy, warm nostalgic tone`
- Bridge deadpan not landing → add `spoken word, comedic timing, deadpan pause between words`
- Chorus not catchy enough → add `simple, chantable hook, call and response`
