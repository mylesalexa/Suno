# Lights Out Tonight *(retro 80s soft-pop — "First Time in San Juan" lane — viral story anthem)*

*One-line concept: the whole block loses power on a July night — phones go dark, screens go black, everyone walks outside — and what starts as chaos turns into the best night the street has ever had.*

*Original concept for the "First Time in San Juan" production lane: retro 80s soft-pop / yacht rock / Motown-influenced, 110 BPM, warm synths + clean electric piano + jangly guitar. No rap, no 808, no supersaw — full group singalong from the first bar. The narrative follows a real group through a real night.*

---

## Concept & Angle

**The "First Time in San Juan" blueprint:** In spring 2026, a Suno-generated song called "First Time in San Juan" went massively viral — Luke Combs, Jennifer Love Hewitt, O-Town, Puerto Rico's tourism board — all because it sounded earnestly like a cheesy 80s sitcom theme about a vacation. That sound (I–IV–V–VI Motown chord progression, warm electric piano, yacht rock instrumentation, wholesome group singalong) is the template. The joke is that it takes itself completely seriously — zero irony in execution, which is why it lands.

**Why "Lights Out Tonight" is perfect for this lane:**
- A power outage is the most universally experienced chaos → best-night story. Everyone has had this night.
- The cast of characters (Mrs. Patterson next door, thirty people in the park, someone with a battery radio) is sitcom-level charm — it writes its own opening credits sequence.
- "Lights out!" shouted in warm group vocal over electric piano is the TikTok clip the moment it plays.
- The screens-go-dark → people come alive arc is the most relevant emotional thesis of 2026 — delivered earnestly, it lands as celebration, not lecture.

**Four things that make it go viral:**
1. **The stamp is the clip:** "Lights out!" delivered in warm, earnest 80s group-vocal is the lip-sync moment — same energy as "First time in San Juan, mi hijo."
2. **The hook is immediate:** "Lights out tonight — let's go" over electric piano lands before anyone can skip it.
3. **The specifics are the sitcom:** "Mrs. Patterson next door — we'd never really met" and "battery radio playing in the dark" are exactly the kind of warm, earnest neighborhood detail that made San Juan viral.
4. **The key change carries the emotional thesis:** The lift to A major lands on the "put the screens down and actually look" moment — the musical peak and the emotional peak are the same moment.

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** **retro 80s soft-pop / yacht rock** — warm electric piano as the spine, bright jangly clean guitar, light snare and hi-hats, warm synth pads swelling on the chorus. Motown-influenced I–IV–V–VI chord warmth, pentatonic melody, polished and clean. The "First Time in San Juan" production blueprint.

- **Tempo:** **110 BPM** — the San Juan sweet spot; bouncy, not frantic, room for the group singalong.
- **Key:** **G major** (bright, instantly warm); lifts to **A major** for the final chorus (classic 80s key change moment).
- **Vocal:** warm, earnest **male lead** — clean singing throughout, no rap, no sung-rap. Group harmonies enter the pre-chorus, full group singalong on the chorus. The delivery is completely sincere — no irony, no wink. That's the move.

## ⭐ Best Suno prompt
```
Retro 80s soft-pop, yacht rock, 110 BPM, key of G, warm synths, clean electric piano, bright jangly guitar, soft snare, earnest male lead, group singalong chorus, wholesome, cheery, feel-good
```

**Tip:** The entire lane lives in `earnest` + `wholesome` — Suno needs to commit to the sincerity or the charm collapses. `clean electric piano` is the production anchor; if Suno loses it, add `Rhodes electric piano, warm keys` to the style box. `group singalong chorus` is what separates this from a solo ballad — the communal feeling is the point. The screens thesis in the bridge must come out as joy and reunion, not critique — the production sincerity is what delivers that. Keep `rap`, `808`, `EDM`, `progressive house`, and `ironic` in Exclude: any of those pull it out of the lane.

- Going too modern / losing the retro feel → add `1980s production, vintage synth, gated reverb snare, warm tape`
- Electric piano disappearing → add `Rhodes electric piano, warm keys, piano lead`
- Chorus not group enough → add `choir singalong, full ensemble, communal vocals`
- Too slow / losing the bounce → push to 112 BPM; add `bright, bouncy, upbeat`
- Too fast / losing the warmth → pull to 108 BPM; add `warm groove, laid-back bounce`
- Bridge too sparse → add `gentle piano ballad bridge, intimate, warm`
- Key change not landing → add `key change final chorus, modulation, lift`

## Variations
- **Female lead / group cast:** swap `earnest male lead` → `warm female lead` or `mixed group lead, everyone sings` — a full ensemble version where nobody is really the lead feels most like a TV theme.
- **More Motown:** add `Motown soul, walking bass, gospel harmonies, hand claps` — warmer and more soulful, the neighborhood-reunion feeling gets a soul foundation.
- **More yacht rock:** add `smooth guitar solo, California yacht rock, Steely Dan influenced` — leans into adult-contemporary 80s, more sophisticated.
- **Winter version:** swap `July` in verse 1 to `January`, `city glow` stars to `snow reflecting the glow` — the power-outage-in-winter story has different specific charm (candles in the cold, everyone wrapped in blankets).
- **Acoustic / campfire:** `acoustic guitar, warm, sing-along, campfire` — strips it down to the earnest core, perfect for an intimate promo or lyric video.
- **Kids chorus version:** add `children's choir, innocent, playful` — goes full Saturday morning cartoon; the neighborhood-comes-together arc plays beautifully.
- **Title/hook swaps:** "The Night the Lights Went Out," "Put the Screens Down," "Stars Were Always There," "Nobody Planned This."
