## Research — Texas Country Sound

- **Instrumentation:** Texas country leans on **steel guitar and fiddle** and traces its roots to frontier dance-hall music, where fiddles and guitars carried melody and rhythm; red dirt bands are often described as needing a fiddle and a steel guitar. ([Wikipedia: Texas country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_country_music) · [Steel Guitar Forum](https://bb.steelguitarforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=263941&sid=d626396e8cdf689e9a1457872c1506db) · [Wikipedia: Red dirt music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_dirt_music))
- **Feel and themes:** it's described as closer to the 80s-90s neo-traditional sound of George Strait than to polished Nashville production, pairing a "common working man" theme with **witty undertones**. ([Critical Country](https://criticalcountry.medium.com/the-exciting-current-landscape-of-country-music-9a8e776916df) · [Wikipedia: Texas country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_country_music))
- **Why this lane now:** country dominates the current charts ("Choosin' Texas" at #1 for about 27 weeks, and country holding the top three), as found in the earlier research. ([Billboard: Song of the Summer](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/ella-langley-choosin-texas-song-of-the-summer-2026-1236335495/))

**Honest limits:** no source covered two-step rhythm or lyric imagery, so the **100 BPM, A major, and the caliche road, water tower, live oak and Fourth of July details are my own choices** from general knowledge. The Texas-specific sound recipe (fiddle, steel, shuffle, twangy Telecaster) is sourced only at the level of "steel and fiddle, dance-hall roots, Strait-like."

## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** One realization, told with Texas details. The verses build the world (bus stop, creek, tire swing, a caliche road). The pre-chorus is the Fourth-of-July tailgate where it turns. The chorus pins the whole thing to place ("Right here in Texas, right here the whole time"). The bridge names the real risk (twenty years of friendship), and the outro closes the loop when the best friend repeats the waitress's joke.

**Four things that make it work in this lane:**
1. **The hook has the place in it,** so the title is searchable and singable.
2. **Dance-hall-ready structure:** a steady shuffle with a sing-along "oh-oh-oh" a crowd can join.
3. **Wit in the working-man tradition:** the waitress who "filled it up" is the quiet joke.
4. **The callback ending:** "Took you long enough" lands twice, once from a stranger and once from the person it matters most to.

**Clean and warm:** no profanity, nothing heavy.
