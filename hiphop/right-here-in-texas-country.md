## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** One realization, told with Texas details and a wink of regret. The verses build the world and admit he was slow ("Dumb as a fencepost — I know that for sure"). The pre-chorus is the Fourth-of-July tailgate where it turns. The chorus pins it to place and lands the bittersweet line ("...and I drove right on by"). The bridge is the risk of saying it, and the outro closes the loop when the best friend repeats the waitress's joke.

**Four things that make it work in this lane:**
1. **Contrast:** a breezy two-step under a story about years of missing something obvious, the same sad-lyric/danceable-groove trick the described sound is built on, at friends-to-lovers scale.
2. **The guitar hook is a character:** it's in the intro, the pre-chorus lead-in, every chorus, and a solo break.
3. **Relaxed, conversational verses:** playful storytelling that opens into an easy sing-along ("Oh-oh-oh").
4. **The callback ending:** "Took you long enough" lands twice.

**Clean and warm:** no profanity; the sass is mild.

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** **retro 70s-80s honky-tonk country-pop with a Texas two-step**: twangy electric guitar hook, pedal steel fills, rolling bass, steady live drums, acoustic strum underneath.

- **Tempo:** **100 BPM** (my choice), a laid-back two-step sway.
- **Key:** **A major**; the final chorus lifts a step.
- **Vocal:** relaxed, drawling **male lead**: conversational and playful on the verses, easy and melodic on the chorus; gang vocals on the "oh-oh-oh"s. No heavy vocal processing.
