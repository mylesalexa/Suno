# Right Here the Whole Time *(stomp-and-clap country-folk-pop — small-town friends-to-lovers, no name)*

*One-line concept: you've known her since you were nine: the same bus stop, the same creek, the first call you make every time. Then she laughs on a tailgate under fireworks and the whole town looks new. A stomp-and-clap, banjo-and-pedal-steel country-folk-pop singalong about falling for your best friend, with no name in it anywhere.*

## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** One realization, told through small-town details. The verses build the world (bus stop, creek, tire swing, one stoplight). The pre-chorus is the moment it turns ("Wait — hold on — how did I miss it?"). The chorus is the answer ("You were right here, right here the whole time"). The bridge names the risk of friends-to-lovers ("Twenty years is a lot to put on the line"), and the outro pays off the waitress's earlier joke when the best friend says it back.

**Four things that make it work in this lane:**
1. **Country imagery over generic romance:** tailgate, riding shotgun, gravel road, one stoplight, a diner counter.
2. **A sing-along that needs no words:** "Oh-oh-oh" fits the stomp-and-clap feel and lets a crowd join.
3. **A hook line that works as a caption:** "Right here the whole time."
4. **The callback ending:** the waitress's "Took you long enough" returns from the best friend's mouth.

**Clean and warm:** no profanity, nothing heavy; the only tension is whether to say it.

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** **modern country-folk-pop**: acoustic guitar, stomp-claps, banjo, pedal steel, a big gang sing-along chorus.

- **Tempo:** **104 BPM**, a relaxed two-step stomp (my choice).
- **Key:** **D major**; the final chorus lifts a step.
- **Vocal:** warm, conversational **male lead** on the verses; gang vocals on the "oh-oh-oh"s and the "hey" shouts.
- **Length:** compact (lyrics are 2158 characters), with the hook arriving in the first line.
