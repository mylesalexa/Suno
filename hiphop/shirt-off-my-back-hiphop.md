# Shirt Off My Back *(clean male-lead melodic hip-hop — fall romance devotion anthem)*

*One-line concept: a gentleman gives a cold girl his sweater on an October night without a second thought — and the idiom "shirt off my back" comes back to its literal origin as a hip-hop love song about genuine, instinctive devotion.*

*Adapted from: `hiphop/shirt-off-my-back-country-pop.md` — same concept and story arc, rebuilt for melodic R&B hip-hop: warm 808 + Rhodes piano replace acoustic guitar + pedal steel; verses shift to melodic sung-rap flow; bridge becomes a rap delivery with a hard contrast line.*

---

## Concept & Angle

**Concept:** "She was cold — I gave her mine" is still the anchor, but the production and delivery change everything. Where the country version is conversational storytelling, the hip-hop version is introspective and emotionally layered — the same gesture, but told from inside his head. The bridge's new hard contrast line — *"some dudes run when it gets cold — I run closer"* — is the hip-hop thesis statement. "Still would!" stays the stamp. Same devotion, different world.

**Four things that make it chart:**
1. **The stamp is still iron:** "Still would!" — two words, present-tense devotion, clips in under two seconds, lives on a TikTok POV with no context needed.
2. **"A gentleman I know"** — she says this to her girls weeks later. In a hip-hop song it hits even harder: chivalry as flex without being a flex.
3. **The bridge contrast lands hard:** "Some dudes run when it gets cold — I run closer." That line is the whole song's argument in one breath.
4. **The chorus is the same across both versions** — deliberately. "No big gesture, nothing grand / Just love the way it ought to land" works in country and hip-hop because the truth in it is genre-agnostic.

**Why it's distinct from the catalog:**
- Not a party song (No Plans, Grand Beach Friday) — quiet, specific, romantic.
- Not summer (Gone Too Fast) — October, fall, warmth in the cold.
- Not generic romance — the sweater is the detail that keeps it from floating away.
- **The only hip-hop love song in the catalog with a male POV devotion narrative** — the anti-flex flex: the shirt off my back is the move, not the chain.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** clean **melodic R&B hip-hop** — warm 808 bass as the spine, Rhodes piano throughout, soft rolling hi-hats, a subtle string swell on the chorus. Melodic sung-rap on verses (rhythmic, pitched, emotionally conversational), full melodic singing on the chorus. The Drake/J. Cole romantic lane — introspective, specific, warm, never gritty.

- **Tempo:** **88 BPM** — hip-hop love song sweet spot; head-nodding groove, room for the emotion.
- **Key:** **G major** (warm, uplifting); no key change — the production swells carry the final chorus.
- **Vocal:** warm **male lead** — melodic sung-rap on verses (conversational but rhythmic), full singing on chorus, wide harmonies on the final drop. Second voice joins bridge call-and-response. Clean throughout.

## ⭐ Best Suno prompt
```
Melodic R&B hip-hop, clean, 88 BPM, key of G, warm 808 bass, Rhodes piano, soft hi-hats, melodic sung-rap verses, emotional male lead, sung chorus, warm harmonies, romantic, genuine, autumn
```

**Tip:** `warm 808 bass` + `Rhodes piano` is the spine — they define the Drake/J. Cole romantic lane. `melodic sung-rap verses` keeps the delivery rhythmic but pitched, not monotone. `trap` and `heavy 808` in Exclude are critical — they pull the 808 away from warm and toward gritty, which kills the romantic register. `88 BPM` is the sweet spot; don't push above 95 or the emotional register shifts toward party.

- Verses going monotone → add `tuneful, pitched rap, melodic flow, emotional delivery`
- 808 too heavy/trap → strengthen Exclude: add `aggressive, drill, dark`; check `heavy 808` is excluded
- Chorus not melodic enough → add `sung hook, wide harmonies, emotional chorus`
- Bridge not rap enough → add `rap bridge, half-time delivery, spoken word bridge`
- Too slow/sleepy → push to 92 BPM; add `driving groove, warm bounce`
- Too generic R&B → add `hip-hop storytelling verses, conversational flow, specific detail`

## Variations
- **Female POV version:** swap `emotional male lead` → `warm female lead`; remove `female lead` from Exclude — her receiving the sweater, remembering it, wearing it for weeks. Completely different emotional frequency, equally strong.
- **Duet:** swap lead → `male-female duet, trade verses` — his verse, her chorus; the bridge already has the call-and-response DNA.
- **Country version:** see `shirt-off-my-back-country-pop.md` — same concept at 96 BPM, acoustic guitar + pedal steel, traditional Nashville production.
- **More trap/darker:** add `melodic trap, 808 sub bass, dark R&B`; remove `trap` from Exclude — moves it toward the The Weeknd lane, more bittersweet.
- **More classic R&B:** pull to 75 BPM; add `soul R&B, live drums, Motown influence, warm bass guitar` — moves it toward old-school devotion song territory.
- **Acoustic / stripped promo:** `88 BPM, acoustic guitar, piano, male vocal, intimate, warm, no production` — same lyrics, stripped for a promo or lyric video.
