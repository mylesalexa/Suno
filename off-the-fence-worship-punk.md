# Off the Fence

*Christian worship pop-punk — the altar call. A direct, second-person invitation to the one still
standing on the fence: Jesus has been knocking your whole life, the door's wide open, and you don't have
to have it figured out first — just come. Anthemic and soaring with a wide-open, arms-out chorus and an
emotional "moment of decision" bridge. Urgent but never condemning.*

---

## Creative Direction

**Topic:** *The altar call — "why are you waiting on the fence when Jesus is calling?"* It speaks straight
to the listener who's heard it all, felt the pull, and kept one foot out. The whole song is the gentle,
relentless invitation to stop wavering and step across — today.

**Why it works (and how it stays warm, not pushy):** the power of an altar call is *kindness with
urgency*. So the verses meet the fence-sitter with understanding ("you think you're too far gone")
instead of guilt, the chorus is **come-as-you-are** ("you don't have to clean up first"), and the prodigal
imagery means God is the one *running* toward them. The urgency ("this is the day") is about love that
won't give up, not fear. It's the companion to "Lovers of the Light" — that one threw the door open; this
one reaches through it and offers a hand.

**Bible verses:**
1. **1 Kings 18:21** — "How long will you waver between two opinions?" *(the fence — the entire premise)*
2. **2 Corinthians 6:2** — "Now is the time of God's favor, now is the day of salvation." *(the chorus — "this is the day")*
3. **Revelation 3:20** — "Here I am! I stand at the door and knock." *(the pre-chorus — He's calling)*
4. **Luke 15:20** — "while he was still a long way off... his father ran to him." *(verse 2 — He runs to you)*
5. **John 6:37** — "whoever comes to me I will never drive away." *(come-as-you-are — no cleanup first)*
6. **Revelation 22:17** — "The Spirit and the bride say, 'Come!'... let the one who is thirsty come." *(pre-chorus 2)*
7. **Romans 10:13** — "everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved." *(the bridge — "one yes")*
8. **Matthew 11:28** — "Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest." *(the invitation)*

**Emotional arc:** to the fence-sitter, one foot in, won't step out (V1) → "how long will you waver? He's
knocking" (pre-chorus) → wide-open inviting chorus: "why you waitin'? just come, this is the day" (chorus)
→ "you think you're too far gone — but He runs to you" (V2) → "no more someday — the Spirit says come"
(pre-chorus 2) → the moment of decision: "one step, one yes, everything changes" (bridge) → huge final
chorus → tender crowd outro: "come on home — don't walk away."

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** anthemic, emotive pop-punk altar call — soaring and wide open. Driving guitars and octave-lead
melody, a big arms-out gang-vocal chorus that feels like an invitation, and a bridge that pulls back to
the intimate "moment" before the final explosion. Urgent energy, warm heart.

- **Tempo:** ~**148–152 BPM** — driving backbeat with claps, room for the choir-style chorus to soar.
- **Key:** **G** major, lift to **A** for the final chorus.
- **Vocal:** passionate, soaring male lead — earnest and direct in the verses, wide and pleading-but-warm
  in the chorus. Gang vocals on the "why you waitin' / just come / this is the day" hooks; the bridge gets
  intimate and almost spoken before the band slams back in.
