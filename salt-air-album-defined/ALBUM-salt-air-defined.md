# SALT AIR: DEFINED — the strict pressing

*A disciplined, strict 14-track re-pressing of the surf-bum album, built after an audit found the original
`surf-bum/` album (14 tracks, "Salt & Static") wasn't strictly true to form. This folder exists
specifically to be that strict version: dawn patrol, slow, lo-fi, surf-shop life, beachfront LA specifics,
and the real texture of surfing — nothing upbeat, nothing anthemic, nothing without tape grain. First
trimmed to a tight 10; then rebuilt back up to 14 with four new tracks researched directly from real surf
technique and etiquette (duck diving, lineup priority rules, board-ding repair, and the actual wind science
behind why dawn is glassy) instead of repeating the upbeat tracks that got cut. Two found-audio devices
carry the whole record: a grainy weather-report opener and a grainy local-newscast interview.
Cloudlight Proxy.*

> **Brief:** "ensure that this album is completely separate from the others... true to form. Dawn patrol.
> Slow. Lofi. Surf shop. Beachfront LA. All the nuances of surfing... if not [strict], make a new folder...
> pile the new revised songs in there. With that grainy weather report opener on a song. Newscast interview
> on another. Slightly odd type music but chill vibes." Then: "Where did our 14 tracks go? Make 4 new ones
> to fill in. Research surfing nuance."

---

## The audit — what failed strict form
Checked all 14 tracks in `surf-bum/` against the brief. Three problems, confirmed directly against each
track's actual style box:

1. **Missing lo-fi texture entirely.** `board-break-instrumental-surf.md`, `tiki-torch-nights-exotica-surf.md`,
   and `night-swim-dreamy-surf.md` had no cassette hiss, crackle, or tape warble in their main style boxes
   at all — despite the album claiming to be "lo-fi throughout."
2. **Too upbeat/anthemic for "slow, chill, odd."** `small-craft-advisory-lofi-surf.md` (112 BPM, "upbeat
   breezy... playful"), `shop-dog-reef-lofi-surf.md` (108 BPM, "bright bouncy... fun"),
   `not-on-any-map-lofi-surf.md` (104 BPM, "gang vocal chants... proud, upbeat"), and
   `building-swell-lofi-surf.md` (118 BPM, "gang vocals... dramatic") all read as pop-anthem energy, not
   dawn-patrol chill.
3. **No specific beachfront-LA grounding.** The original was set in a generic "beach town" — no coast
   highway, no marine layer, no lifeguard towers, no pier. Real, not vague.

---

## What this pressing does differently
- **Every track's style box now has explicit lo-fi/tape texture** — no exceptions, including the one
  fast track (Board Break keeps its 152 BPM vintage surf-rock speed, which is period-authentic, but now
  carries cassette hiss and vinyl crackle like everything else).
- **Tempo held strictly in the 66–104 BPM range**, except that one flagged, deliberate outlier.
- **Small Craft Advisory rewritten** slower (112 → 84 BPM) and deadpan instead of playful — still opens on
  the grainy weather report, now delivered dry rather than hyped.
- **Forty Summers' intro reframed** from a casual street interview into a grainy **local-newscast**
  segment (anchor voice-over, then the interview clip) — this is the newscast-interview track.
- **Four tracks cut entirely** for being too far from strict form: Shop Dog, Not on Any Map, Building
  Swell, and Fish Tacos & Empty Pockets. They're not bad songs — they just belong to the looser, more
  varied `surf-bum/` album, not this tightly-defined one. Untouched there.
- **Four new tracks added back in**, researched from real surf technique/culture instead of repeating the
  cut tracks' upbeat energy — see below.
- **Beachfront-LA specifics woven in**: marine layer / "June gloom," Pacific coast highway, lifeguard
  towers, the pier — real, recurring, not generic "beach."

## Loose Jam sections — every track, added after the fact
The tracks were coming out lyric-heavy — every section had words, with barely any room to just sit in a
groove. Added a `[Loose Jam]`-tagged instrumental section to all 14 tracks, always dropped into a spot the
song already had room to wander (an existing breakdown, bridge, or tape-warp moment) rather than bolted on
awkwardly. Each one is purely instrumental — no lyrics, no drug references, just hazy, spacious,
unhurried playing that lengthens the song and gives it room to breathe. Night Swim's stays deliberately
quiet, matching that track's own no-big-lift rule; Board Break's keeps some drive since it's the album's
one energetic outlier; everything else just wanders. If a generated take still feels too word-dense, the
fix is the same everywhere: extend that track's `[Loose Jam]` tag further, or add `long instrumental
outro, extended jam` to the Style of Music box.

**A second wander, right after the first chorus, on six tracks:** Salt Air, Duck Dive, Small Craft
Advisory, Tiki Torch Nights, Night Swim, and The Glass each got an additional `[Loose Jam — first drift]`
section inserted between the first chorus and Verse 2 — before the story even gets moving. These are the
six most "vibe-first" tracks on the record; the more plot-driven ones (Ding Repair, Forty Summers, Open Til
the Swell Comes In, The Lineup, Flat Spell, Home Movie, Board Break) were left with just their one existing
jam so the story keeps momentum. So most of these six tracks now wander twice — once right after the
first chorus, once later in the bridge/breakdown — which is the real way to drag the vibe out rather than
stretching a single jam section unnaturally long.

## The four new tracks — researched, not invented
Each one is built on a real, verified piece of surf technique or culture, not just a mood:

- **Duck Dive** (Track 2) — the actual technique for getting through the impact zone: start the dive 5–7
  feet before the wave hits, push the nose under with the rails, drive a knee onto the tail so the whole
  board submerges evenly, forming one arc from air to water and back.
- **The Lineup** (Track 3) — real etiquette: the surfer closest to the peak has priority, nobody "snakes"
  around someone to steal a wave, new surfers start on the shoulder and work in, and if you blow a wave you
  go to the back of the line, not straight back to the front.
- **Ding Repair** (Track 8) — the real resin-repair ritual: clean the board of wax and sand, check for
  trapped water, apply resin, press out bubbles, cover in plastic to hold the shape, and cure out of direct
  sun — small dings need at least an hour, full cure runs several hours to a full day.
- **The Glass** (Track 10) — the actual wind science behind dawn patrol: land cools faster than the ocean
  at night, so cool air sinks and flows offshore, smoothing the wave face into glass; as the sun heats the
  land through the morning, the wind flips onshore and the glass turns to chop, usually by late morning.

## Relationship to `surf-bum/`
This is a **separate, stricter pressing**, not a replacement. `surf-bum/ALBUM-salt-and-static.md` (14
tracks) stays exactly as it was — a looser, more varied record that still holds together well on its own
terms, just not to this album's strict brief. This folder, `salt-air-album-defined/`, is the tight cut:
every track slow-to-mid tempo (bar one flagged exception), every track lo-fi in its actual style box, every
track grounded in a specific beachfront-LA setting, and exactly two found-audio devices instead of four —
now back to the same 14-track length, filled with real technique and etiquette instead of upbeat filler.
