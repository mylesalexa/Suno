# When the Sky Turns Green

*Dark, driving country-rock — a storm song in the lineage of muscled-up, ominous Nashville rockers like
"Ain't No Love in Oklahoma." The tornado is both literal and a force of pitiless judgment: out on the
southern prairie, the wind doesn't love anybody — it takes the roof, the truck, the home, and her.
Heavy electric guitars, a stomping beat, a gritty raspy baritone, and a hook built to grip.*

> **Original — not a copy.** Captures the *feel* of dark storm-country (menace, fatalism, the storm as
> a living thing) with entirely original lyrics and imagery. No artist names go in the Suno box.

---

## Creative Direction

**Concept:** *On the southern plains, the storm shows no mercy — "when the sky turns green, you make your
peace."* A man watches a supercell drop on everything he built. The tornado is the antagonist and the
metaphor: an indifferent, almost biblical force that takes without asking. The grip comes from the turn —
it didn't just take the house, it took *her*.

**Why it grips like the reference (without copying):**
- **Dread before disaster.** The verses live in the eerie *calm before* — still air, animals fleeing, the
  green wall cloud — so the chorus hits like the storm itself.
- **The storm as a character with no love in it.** Same emotional engine as the reference (a place/force
  that offers no mercy), but our hook and images are our own: *"the wind don't love nobody," "the dirt
  don't forgive a thing."*
- **Authentic, specific detail** (researched): the green sky, hail like nails, the freight-train roar,
  the concrete slab left where a house stood, "you can't outrun it." Specificity is what makes it real.
- **A personal gut-punch.** "It took the only home we built, and it took her" turns a weather song into
  a grief song — that's the lump-in-the-throat lift.

**Emotional arc:** the unnatural calm and the green sky (V1) → hail and the roar, no time left (pre) →
the storm's verdict: it takes everything, make your peace (chorus) → the morning after, the missing field,
the missing her (V2) → cursing a sky that won't answer (pre 2) → fatalist bridge: you can't reason with it,
the prairie always collects (bridge) → final chorus → ominous fade.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal

**Genre:** dark, driving country-rock — heavy and cinematic. Big distorted electric guitars and a menacing
slide/dobro line over a stomping floor-tom beat; Hammond organ swells for dread; gritty, powerful raspy
baritone. Brooding verses that detonate into a huge, ominous chorus. Southern-gothic storm energy.

- **Tempo:** ~**136–142 BPM** — a heavy mid-tempo stomp, relentless, not frantic.
- **Key:** **E minor** (dark), with the chorus opening big and menacing rather than bright.
- **Vocal:** gritty, weathered, powerful male baritone with a raspy edge — restrained and ominous in the
  verses, full-throated and desperate in the chorus. Optional low gang shout doubling the hook line.

Every cow in the back forty turned and started home.
Radio's cuttin' in and out, Mama's countin' down the miles,
That wall cloud's wearin' a color I ain't trusted in a while.

### Chorus
When the sky turns green and the sirens scream,

Out here the wind don't love nobody,
It'll take the roof, it'll take the body,

Down where the dirt don't forgive a thing,
