# Salt Air *(retro surf-bum pop — sun-soaked, almost meaningless, lo-fi)*

*A clean, breezy **surf-bum retro** anthem about absolutely nothing important — wax on the board, salt in
the hair, no watch, no plans, just the wave and the sun. Researched from **"Something in the Water" by

sunlit, intentionally unpolished — the song itself slows down in the middle, like floating, before paddling

## Researching the lane
*(artist/album names are filtered in Suno, so these become sound descriptors, not names)*
- **PARTY WAVE / "Something in the Water" / Dawn Patrol** → a modern Christian surf duo (Forrest Frank +
  Noah Hayden) built on real early-morning surf sessions ("dawn patrol"). Their sound is deliberately
  **stripped back, sun-soaked, and intentionally unpolished** — "refreshingly unpolished," room for the
  songs to breathe, not overproduced. The album itself is paced like a day unfolding: starts slow, builds,
  ends in reflection and rest — that pacing idea is where the mid-song tempo drop comes from.
- **Classic 60s surf-rock** (Dick Dale / Beach Boys / Ventures lane, translated to descriptors) → reverb-
  drenched **tremolo-picked guitar**, twangy surf licks, loose brushed drums, warm harmonies — the "retro"
  half of the ask.
- **The fusion, plainly:** classic surf-guitar twang and reverb for the retro texture, Party-Wave's warm
  lo-fi sun-soaked looseness for the modern feel, and a lyric sheet that's intentionally light — no plot, no
  lesson, just salt, sun, and a board. Clean means no profanity, not "safe" — it's still cool.

## Concept & Angle
**Concept:** a song about nothing, on purpose. No story, no moral, no arc — just the physical feeling of an
early surf session: waxing the board, paddling out, losing track of time, floating. **The structural trick:**
the song itself slows down in the middle — a stripped-back, half-time "floating" breakdown (just guitar and
voice, tape-warble warmth) — before the drums and bass paddle back in for the final chorus. It mirrors the
actual rhythm of a surf session: paddle, float, catch it.

---

## Style / Tempo / Vocal
**Genre:** **retro surf-bum pop** — reverb-drenched tremolo surf guitar, warm lo-fi sun-soaked production,
loose brushed drums, handclaps, breezy stripped-back arrangement. Intentionally unpolished — room to
breathe, not radio-glossy.

- **Tempo:** ~**104 BPM** main groove, **dropping to a loose half-time float** in the breakdown before
  building back to full tempo.
- **Key:** **A major** (bright, warm, guitar-friendly).
- **Vocal:** laid-back, breezy, **unpolished lead** (male or female) — almost talk-sung on the verses, warm
  and easy on the chorus, loose group harmonies. Clean, casual, unbothered. (Duet/harmony swap noted.)

**Tip:** the two load-bearing phrases are `tremolo reverb guitar` (the retro-surf half) and `warm sun-soaked
lo-fi production... stripped back` (the modern unpolished half) — keep both. `tempo drop breakdown` is what
tells Suno to actually slow the middle section down; if it stays at one tempo the whole way, add `half-time
floating breakdown, tempo change` explicitly. Too polished → add `unpolished, room to breathe, loose take`.
Too sleepy → nudge to 108 BPM or add `bigger drums on the final chorus`.
